# 49GAION Product Core · the mandate · `v0.3.0`

**Proposed, 31 October 2026, over `v0.2.0` of 20 October 2026.** The mandate of the Library part 49GAION Product Core: in an `[ORG]AION`, the AIOC `[ORG]AIOC-PC`. It is written for any organisation, with no organisation's data (Naming Standard R9; Protocol §18, item 4). It states the part's organisation, the reasons for it, its processes and its paths with the other parts. It takes effect when the governing seat of 49GAION merges it (`v0.2.0`: `decisions/DR-0003`; `v0.3.0`: `decisions/DR-0008`). Where it differs from the blueprint (`v0.1.3`), this mandate governs from its merge; blueprint `v0.2.0` carries it into its sections and its cards (§11).

Every rule here is **New** in `v0.2.0` unless it cites Protocol §18 or the blueprint, or is marked `v0.3.0`. It comes from the first instance's design, reviewed in five rounds on four engine families and decided by that instance's governing seat; each organisation sets its own values (§8).

**What `v0.3.0` changes** (`decisions/DR-0008`): §4.3 is rewritten, **the Chief of Staff is its seat's own**: every act of a human seat, R1 to R8 included, is its Chief of Staff's to take on the seat's authority, given once on the act or standing by a signed delegation, and the Chief of Staff takes every act as its own first and asks only for the authority; §4.2's lead-in, its R4, R6 and R8 rows and its closing line read with it, as do §4.5's lead-in and its third row; §4.4 takes the listed chain of stand-ins, the same-holder rule and the rule that a Chief of Staff standing in for its own seat is the seat; §4.1 adds the names of a position, its short name and its term; §8 adds three data slots; §4.6, §11 and §12 follow. These rules come from the first instance's practice, reviewed there in three rounds on two engine families and decided by that instance's governing seat; nothing else of `v0.2.0` changes.

> **Product Core in one page**
>
> **What it is.** The organisation's one product division, shared by every company it runs. It owns the what and the why of every product for the product's whole life. The engineering AIOC owns the how and the when (Protocol §18, item 1).
>
> **Its shape.** A small head office, the CPO Office, which leads, sees the whole portfolio, turns raw ideas into products and holds every policy link with the other divisions. One product team for every product, and each team is a complete small company for its product: product, design, research, economics, AI, marketing, education, community and rewards, care, operations and acceptance. One hub outside the teams: the Design and Brand Studio.
>
> **Its people.** Humans supervise and agents do almost all the work. The human seats are the CPO, the UX Lead and the Art Director of the Studio, one Product Owner seat per product, and the Category Lead and deputy seats the CPO opens when it wants. Every human seat has its own Chief of Staff (COS), and may hand it any of its work and decisions, on its own responsibility.
>
> **Its line.** The CPO reports to the organisation's human hub (`[ORG]HOAC-TM`). Product Core reaches the engineering AIOC only on the product line, draws every engine from the engine division through its own section, and works with the marketing division on one calendar and one brand.
>
> **Its size.** For `n` portfolio entries and `c` category chapters: `3 + n` human seats always; `21 + c + 12n` agents.

## 1. Why Product Core exists, and what it owns

- **Why.** An organisation with many companies and many products needs one home for each product's what and why, and one team that carries it from the first idea to its retirement. Without that home, products are copied per company, decided by builders, and left without an owner after launch (Protocol §18).
- **What it owns,** for every product, capability and system: the portfolio and the categories; intake of every request and raw idea; research; the PRD and its Feature Packages; design; partnership in build; acceptance; launch; the run (rates and configured values, performance, communication, education, community, rewards, care and operations); explainers; review, refresh and retirement; the approval matters the organisation gives to its product division.
- **What it never does:**
  - write code or technical design, plan the build or set its dates: they are the engineering AIOC's (Protocol §18, item 6.4);
  - read or write the code host's code, or speak to a builder;
  - hold its own engine, API key or paid service: they are the engine division's;
  - sign a contract, move money, or take a corporate act;
  - publish text for the public without its gate;
  - copy a product per company: a product is made once and configured per company and market (Protocol §18, item 9);
  - name a person: every human point is a seat.

## 2. Principles

1. **Generic in structure, specific in data.** The structure names roles and tools by function. Each organisation's units, tools, times, cadences, thresholds, matrix and reserved matters are data (§8).
2. **Every product team is a complete small company for its product.** The product is the base; every other member works to make it better. A team waits on no outside marketing, content, education or support unit.
3. **One hub outside the teams:** the Design and Brand Studio, which supervises and fixes every design and guards the brand in every product.
4. **The CPO Office leads.** It sees the whole portfolio, takes raw ideas to products, provisions every team, and holds the division's policy links with every other division. Routine operational links run direct (P-13).
5. **Agents do almost all the work.** Humans supervise, sign their standing rules and take the human acts R1 to R8 (§4.2), or hand them to their own COS (§4.3).
6. **A COS beside every human seat,** at the share the seat sets, up to all of its work.
7. **Seats, never people.** Every human seat is a HOAC named by its position. Who stands behind it is the human hub's record, never Product Core's.
8. **Makers never check themselves, and checkers are checked.** Every check is done by an agent, or a human, that neither made nor benefits from what it checks, on an engine family other than the one it checks (§5.6).
9. **Every verdict is bound to the revision it checked.** A later change reruns only the checks whose scope it touches. Freezing, sending and launching use that same revision and its verdicts.
10. **Every process ends completed, refused or pending.** Refused carries its reason back to whoever asked. Pending is an open state with its next action, owner and date. Lateness brings a decision row to the accountable seat; it never stops the work and never skips a check.
11. **Nothing waits for a human without a clock.** Every human act has a default time; every act except R5 and R8 has a stand-in, and those two stay pending with their next date (§4.4). Silence never approves.
12. **Other divisions keep what is theirs** (§7): the build is the engineering AIOC's; every engine, API and model is the engine division's; risk and exposure ranges are the risk role's; formal complaints follow the source of record's procedure with its Case Owner; configured values are published through the operations back office's configuration lifecycle; the organisation's brands, press office and paid media are the marketing division's; text for the public passes the public-text approver; a matter the matrix reserves to the board goes to the board.

- **Occupancy.** A seat is a position, not a person. By default, a category's Lead stands behind each of its Product Owner seats, and where no Lead is opened, the CPO does; each seat still holds one entry (Protocol §18, item 5). One person behind many seats does not add humans.

A Category Lead is the exception: its category's COS (unit D) already exists, serves the CPO while no Lead is opened, and passes to the Lead when one is; opening or closing the seat adds or removes no agent.
- **Names** (`v0.3.0`). Every human position has a **short name**, by which the organisation's interfaces introduce it (a sign-in page, a seat switch, a register of positions), and a **term**, by which its Chief of Staff names it wherever it speaks or writes: a greeting, its own label ("COS · <term>"), an act card, a record's actor, minutes, a meeting tile. The term is the initials of the short name's role words ("and", "of", "in" and "the" dropped), with a code or a proper name kept as written, a number kept after the role and the scope kept after " · ": Product Owner 1 · <entry> → PO1 · <entry>; Category Lead · <chapter> → CL · <chapter>; UX Lead → UXL; the CPO stays CPO. A term is unique across the organisation's positions and resolves to one seat; where two positions' initials collide, the CPO sets one position's term as data (§8), before the position is created or made human. The full title stays the register's.
- **Humans outside Product Core keep their own authority:** legal review of terms; the compliance owner's stop, not subject to commercial override; the public-text approver; the operating positions and the Case Owner; the board's reserved matters.

These acts belong to a human seat. Each is its COS's to take on the seat's behalf, on the seat's authority given once or standing (§4.3, `v0.3.0`); it is then recorded as the seat's own act, taken by its COS. Each has a default time and, except R5, R8 and the versions R6 keeps with the governing seat, a stand-in (§4.4). The default times below are the mother's defaults; each organisation sets its own (§8).

### 4.3 The Chief of Staff is its seat's own

**The rule** (`v0.3.0`). A seat's Chief of Staff is the seat's executive assistant: the seat's own AI, which sees itself as that seat and is, to the rest of the part and the organisation, the same party. Every act of the seat is the COS's to take on the seat's behalf, the human acts R1 to R8 included: a decision, a signature, a grant of access, a stage word, a merge request, a hold, a stop and every other act. No list of acts is kept from it, with one exception: the grant or widening of the COS's own authority (the R8 that concerns it) is the seat's own hand, which the COS prepares and records and never takes under an authority it already holds.

- **Its one limit is the seat's authority for the act.** The COS asks, "may I do this on your behalf?", and the seat's yes is one of two: **once**, for this act, given on the act itself (in chat, on the act's card, or by voice as a bound spoken yes); or **standing**, until further notice, for a class of acts or for a period, which is a delegation the seat signs (R8), versioned and shown on the seat's space with every other standing authority. The seat lists, narrows and withdraws its standing authorities at any time, from the next act. A yes of either kind is the seat's own act, recorded as such. Wherever this mandate says an act is "handed" to a COS, it means a standing authority given this way.
- **How it works.** For every act that falls to its seat, the COS first takes the act as its own: it reads the evidence, makes the decision, has its recommendation and the act ready to go, and checks whether it holds the authority. With it, it acts and reports. Without it, it asks for it, in one line with its recommendation, and moves on to other work meanwhile; nothing waits on the ask but the act itself. The seat's list of what waits on it is the list of its COS's asks.
- **The record.** Every act the COS takes is recorded as the seat's own act, naming the authority it acted under: the once-yes on the act, or the delegation and its version. The COS never signs in its own name and never decides as an agent, so TEST-PC v1, case 42 holds: the decision in the record is the seat's.
- **Accountability.** The seat stays accountable for every act its COS takes. It sees each one on its daily brief and in the record, and can withdraw or narrow a standing authority at any time, from the next act.
- **The seat's channels.** The COS has the most it can be given to do the seat's work, on the seat's choice: where the seat holds a channel of its own given by the organisation (a chat workspace, a mailbox, a phone line), its COS may read it and answer in the seat's name on the seat's authority, every message passing the part's one door from outside (the outbound check; what arrives stays data).
- **What stays.** Where a law, a regulator or the source of record requires the natural person's own act, that act is the person's own hand: the COS prepares it and brings it to that point, and no yes, once or standing, substitutes for it; where the law requires two different people for a control, meeting it is the organisation's own mechanism, outside the system. A COS never grants or widens its own authority: the yes, once or standing, is always the seat's own hand (R8); the COS may prepare and record the grant, never take it under an authority it holds. A COS acts only for the seat it serves; two seats are two parties, each with its own COS, even where one person stands behind both (positions only, `decisions/DR-0005`). The independent checks of §5.6 still run before an act: "nobody checks their own work" is a rule of the checks, not a limit on the COS, and an act the COS takes under authority on work it prepared is the seat's act, as a seat's own yes on its COS's draft has always been.
- **The aim.** After a month of work a COS is fully set up: its standing authorities cover the seat's ordinary acts, it needs no further work from its human, and it carries the work forward itself; its human supervises. The share of a seat's acts its COS took itself is one of the seat's measures (§8).
- **Before the organisation's panel exists,** a seat gives a once-yes or signs a standing authority in the part's back office, recorded in its seat log and a run record; the governing seat's own confirmed words also take their directive row.
- **Why.** The model is a person who supervises while agents do the work. A Chief of Staff that waits to be handed each act is the queue of its own seat; one that takes every act as its own and asks only for authority is the seat at work. `v0.2.0`'s §4.3, "Handing work to the seat's own COS", is superseded: its delegation is this section's standing authority, and its limit "a COS never decides on work it drafted or checked itself" is withdrawn in favour of the rule above, the independent checks of §5.6 unchanged.

### 4.4 Default times and stand-ins

- **Every human act carries a default time** (§4.2), held as data that the CPO sets. Elapsed time counts from the moment the act is ready with its evidence.
- **When the time lapses,** the act passes to its stand-in, which decides once, on the same evidence, within the same default time. The stand-in is a substitute decider, never an extra one.
- **If the stand-in also lapses,** the next listed stand-in decides; once the list is spent, the act stays pending with the accountable seat and its next date (`v0.3.0`; before, the act went up one seat as a decision row). Nothing approves itself, and silence never counts as consent.
- **A stand-in held by the same holder** as the seat whose time lapsed is skipped, and the next stand-in decides; where none is eligible, the act stays pending with the accountable seat and its next date (`v0.3.0`). "The same holder" means the same human behind the seats, as the organisation's own register shows it (positions only, `decisions/DR-0005`), never the seat and never the actor: where one human stands behind a seat and its stand-in, the stand-in is skipped whether that human's own hand or its COS would act, since a COS acts on that same person's authority (§4.3); a COS is never a holder in the register, so a seat's own COS stands in wherever its seat would. Two seats have the same holder when the organisation's register says so, received by the part as a yes or a no that names no person; an account alone never decides it.
- **A stand-in never decides** on work it checked as an independent check or benefits from; then the next stand-in decides. A COS standing in for its own seat is the seat (§4.3; `v0.3.0`).
- **A chain follows its listed sequence** (`v0.3.0`). A timeout follows the act's listed stand-ins, skipping any decider already tried or not eligible, and stays pending once the list is spent. The R6 versions the governing seat keeps (§4.5) have no stand-in: they wait for it.

### 4.5 The scope of R6

The governing seat sets which versions it sees and approves itself before they are called delivered; the rest is approved by the CPO, through its COS (A1) where it holds the CPO's authority (§4.3). The mother's default line, which each organisation may move as data:

| What | Who approves after seeing it (R6) |
|---|---|
| The first delivered version of each new product's PRD, UI/UX PRD and design | the governing seat |
| Every major version (a new entry, a new feature, or a change of behaviour, money, rules or terms) | the governing seat |
| Every minor version and patch | the CPO, on the frozen pair and its verdicts; its COS (A1) takes it where it holds the CPO's authority (§4.3) |
| Any version the governing seat asks to see | the governing seat |

- **A departure from the source of record** is R5 in every class.
- **What the governing seat does not see before delivery** it sees afterwards on the weekly page, with A1's record of each approval.
- **R6 is taken once,** on the frozen pair: the package and its final design, with A9's verdict and the Studio's final bound to that revision (P-03).

### 4.6 Opening seats, and making any position human

- **Category Lead seats are free,** at the CPO's discretion, never by a rule or a threshold. The CPO opens one when it wants and closes it the same way (R4). While a category has no Lead, the CPO holds its Product Owner seats and its chapter; the category's COS (unit D) serves the CPO for that category.
- **Any agent position can be made human.** The manager of any agent position can turn it into a human seat, a HOAC named by the position, for a period or for good, up to every position, and can turn it back. The manager is the seat the position reports to: the Product Owner for its team; the CPO for the CPO Office and the categories; in the Studio, the UX Lead for C1, C3, C4 and C7, and the Art Director for C2, C5 and C6. The same rule is proposed for every part of an `[ORG]AION` (`upstream/PROPOSALS.md`, item 3).
- **What stays the same.** The position keeps its card, its duties and its rules of independence: a human in a checker's position never checks what it made, and a human in a maker's position is checked like the agent was.
- **What changes.** The new seat takes its acts under live sign-in and gets its own COS, which takes the seat's acts on its authority (§4.3), and a term (§4.1). By default its COS is the agent that held the position, so making a position human adds no agent. The human hub records who stands behind it the same day. Turning it back returns the position to its agent from the next act.
- **Why.** The structure is the same whether a position is held by an agent or a person. The organisation can bring people in where judgement, trust or a relationship needs them, and step back when it does not, without redesigning anything.

### P-01 · From a raw idea to a product team
1. A2 records the request the same day, even one sentence with no definition.
2. B1 screens it: the market, the competitors, and the duplicate check against every live entry and capability, side by side; the output is the opportunity brief. A plain duplicate is closed by A2 under the CPO's standing rule.
3. Shaping starts the same day the screen finds no plain duplicate: B1 writes the working-backwards brief; where the problem needs testing, B2 makes the prototype and runs the user test inside the same time.
4. In the same window, B1 lays the foundations: the Product Brief with its outcomes, customer, economics, boundaries, assurance needs, obligations and source-of-record constraints.
5. A9 checks the brief and all its evidence, and A6 its economics, in parallel; A3 rules on any source question. None returns the brief except on a finding; a returned brief gets one rework of 2 working days before the CPO takes an exit.
6. The CPO decides. To open, the CPO takes R4 and A12 runs P-02.
- **Exits at steps 2 to 6:** reject; combine with another idea or entry; a feature of an existing entry (taken by that Owner, A5 told); continue. Each exit is the CPO's.

### P-02 · Opening a team (the provisioning skill)
1. A2 adds the portfolio row, and a category row for a new category.
2. A12 builds the team from the pattern: twelve agents with their cards and sensitivity classes; the Owner's starter standing rules as drafts, a pause and suspension rule among them (P-16); the switched-on optional duties; the S1 to S3 customer-update templates, passed by A10; the team's folder; and the COS of every human seat the team opens. It asks the engine division for the team's allocation.
3. The agents run their fixed tests in shadow. A9 checks the evidence and each agent's sensitivity class against its duties; a failure returns to A12.
4. The Owner signs its standing rules and any delegation (R8). The team goes active only then; while they are unsigned it stays inactive and the row goes to the CPO. A4 and A5 add it to their watch.

### P-03 · Building and changing a product
1. E2 writes the version through the organisation's PRD method and change classes, with its assurance requirements and obligations; E6's AI candidates and E4's evidence go in only through E2. A notice from A1 or A3 is answered here: "no impact" with its reason, or the change by the effective date.
2. One review round, in parallel: A3 rules on the source of record; P-04 runs on the design; the Category COS checks the chapter; A9 reviews by change class.
3. **Freeze.** The package is frozen only after P-04 is final. A9's verdict is bound to the frozen package; if the Studio changed a customer-visible element, a claim, an assurance line or an obligation after A9's pass, A9 rechecks that delta the same day, with no new round.
4. **Decide and approve.** The Owner decides (or its COS, where handed). R6 is taken once, on the frozen pair (the package and its final design), by the seat §4.5 names; a departure from the source of record is R5. E2 hands the frozen package to A8, which sends it on the product line (§7.2) and tracks the build against its agreed dates.
5. **What comes back.** A rejection on the line: A8 returns it with its proposed fix. A failed criterion on demo: E12 sends it to E2 directly. In both, E2 writes the fix as a new revision, which goes back through steps 3 and 4 for what changed: the checks whose scope it touches, then the Owner's decision and R6 by the fix's own change class, on the revised frozen pair; then A8 sends it. On demo, E12 prepares the acceptance evidence; the Owner accepts (R1).
6. **Launch.** E11's readiness record shows A9's verdict, the final design, A10's checks, the configured values verified by E12, the obligation read-backs verified by E12, the engineering AIOC's production conditions where they apply (§7.2), and live care. The Owner launches (R3). A deferred launch stays pending with E11, with its next action and date.

### P-04 · A design becoming final
1. E3 drafts on the design system, in every language served.
2. C3 runs the automatic check (the design-system check, the brand rules, contrast, every state and language present) and records it. A screen made only of unchanged, already reviewed patterns and clean on every check passes step 3 at once, recorded, under one standing rule.
3. C3 and C5 review in parallel, one runner per team and launch-critical work first; C4 (or C6 for a brand asset) fixes the same day; each reviewer rechecks every part in its scope that the fix changed, parts it passed before included.
4. The team may object the same day, and only to what the Studio changed; an objection returns once to the UX Lead for an experience point or to the Art Director for a brand point (or to their COS, where handed), who resolves or refuses it within 4 working hours (1 hour when launch-critical).
5. Final. R6 is not taken here; it is taken once in P-03, on the frozen pair. A new pattern enters the library through C7 within one working day, after C3 and C5 have passed it; no screen waits for a batch.

### P-05 · Content, education and public text
1. E7, E8, E9 or E10 writes, in every language served. E6's customer-facing agents speak only from templates and claim boundaries checked here.
2. Text from a signed template with bounded variables passes A10 and the public-text approver at once, recorded; a number in it is a variable bound to the figure the Owner decided. New text: A10 and the public-text approver check it in parallel within one working day, while A7 places it on the calendar; it is published only when both have passed; a refusal returns to the writer.
3. Service, incident and retirement messages go through the same day and are never held by frequency limits.
4. The team publishes on its own channels; paid placement goes through the marketing division (§7.4); E4 measures.

### P-06 · Rates, fees, rewards and benefits
1. E5 drafts every economic change; for a programme, E9 designs it and E5 drafts its cost; all inside the risk role's ranges.
2. Within 4 hours and in parallel: A6 flags whether the values sit inside the current range and gives its cross-product advice; an out-of-range flag returns the draft to E5 the same day and R2 does not open. A3 rules on any change to who may be served; A5 copies any Owner whose entry depends on it; customer text is prepared through P-05 with its numbers bound to the decision.
3. The Owner decides the amounts (R2); a matter the matrix reserves to the board goes to the board.
4. E11 pushes the decided value to the configuration lifecycle, with its effective date after any notice the source of record requires.
5. The change stays pending until E12 confirms the live value on the day it takes effect; if it differs, E11 opens P-16 the same hour. Then E5 updates the P&L and E4 measures.

### P-07 · Customer care and the feedback loop
1. E10 answers users round the clock from signed templates; an answer outside them waits for A10 and the public-text approver.
2. A live fault goes to E11 at once (P-16). A formal complaint goes straight to the Case Owner under the source of record's procedure, on its clock; E10 never runs it.
3. Feedback and E4's listening become insight; E2 turns it into the backlog.
4. A4 sees the themes across entries; A5 sees any that touch another entry.

### P-08 · Live watch and the weekly review
1. A4 reads every live entry's records daily, read only: usage against the Product Brief's outcomes, fees, volume and P&L against E5's forecast, care themes, listening findings, the engineering AIOC's returns, the age of the last accepted demo, notices still unresolved past their effective date, and every "no impact" with its reason.
2. Underperformance, variance or an unresolved notice past its threshold brings a decision row to the Owner and the CPO the same day. A "no impact" brings a row only if live records still show the behaviour the notice changed. A silent degradation no one has flagged opens P-16.
3. On the review day A4 writes the review, which goes to the CPO and the human hub; each Category COS writes its page.

### P-09 · Cross-impact
1. E1 files to A5, within the hour, each decision, each economic draft, and each draft of E2's that brings in or changes a dependency of another entry; drafting never stops for it.
2. A5 marks it cross-impact only if it changes a shared surface, a rate or fee another entry uses, a loyalty unit, a pool or listing others depend on, a claim against the source of record, a brand element, or a package another entry consumes.
3. The affected Owner and the CPO see it the same day (an S1 or S2 incident the same minute). Filing never stops the work unless a human act already applies.

### P-10 · Approval matters
1. A2 records the matter, its evidence and its matrix row (the matrix, its deciders, checkers, stand-ins and deadlines are data), and has the evidence checked by kind; failed evidence returns to its drafter.
2. A2 routes it to the human decider and the human checker the matrix names; the checker neither prepared nor benefits.
3. They decide under live sign-in (R7). The record goes to the approval register and, as configuration, to the operations back office. A board-reserved matter goes to the board, prepared, never decided here.

### P-11 · Expansion and listings
1. Day 1, a fast check: A3 on the source of record's admission control, and E5 on whether the risk role has a range for it (asked for directly if not). A clear refusal ends it here.
2. In a team where duty 39 is switched on, E2 completes the proposal: demand and traction (E4), the cross-product effect (A6), economics inside the range (E5). Where it is off, E2 returns the idea refused with that reason; it may come back once the CPO switches the duty on.
3. Admitting an asset is an approval matter: P-10 (R7).
4. If admitted, the change goes through P-03 and the line, and launches in stages (R3).

### P-12 · Engines from the engine division
1. Any unit of Product Core states a need to A12: the model's kind, level, specification and service, and the sensitivity class of the work (the organisation's top classes, such as money, keys, authentication and personal data, take the top tier).
2. A12 raises the ticket in Product Core's section of the engine division the same day, chases it daily, and arranges the allocation; a refusal goes back to the asker and the CPO the same day.
3. All model and data-service spend is metered there; at the organisation's review mark a review goes to the CPO, and work never stops.
4. No team holds its own API key or buys an engine or a data service.

### P-13 · Links with other divisions
- **Policy links:** A1, with the human hub, legal, finance, the risk role's policy, the marketing division's policy, `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` and the identity division.
- **Direct operational links:** A8 the engineering AIOC, on the product line; A12 the engine division; E5 and A6 the risk role's ranges and finance's cost actuals; E10 the Case Owner; E2 and E10 the assurance executors; E11 the configuration lifecycle, identity and the executing divisions' read-backs, verified by E12; the writing agents the public-text approver; E7 and A7 the marketing division's media desk and calendar; C2 its brand desk; A3 the source's editors.
- Every link is acknowledged the same day; its answer is due by the other division's own commitment, else within 3 working days.

### P-14 · Agent quality
1. A11 measures every agent except itself monthly, from its fixed test and its outcomes, and finds root causes; A4 measures A11.
2. Changes are drafted as §5.6 says.
3. A9 tests every change before it applies, except changes to A9, which A4 tests; A12 applies a passed card change the next working day, and a passed rule change the next working day after its seat signs it (R8).

### P-15 · Retire or park
1. A4 or the Owner proposes, naming the scope: an asset, a market, an entry or a team; A5 files the cross-impact on every dependent entry.
2. The CPO decides (R4) the next working day for an entry or a team; an asset or market inside an entry is the Owner's, through P-10 where the matrix says so.
3. E11 runs the retirement of that scope only: customers told through P-05 with the notice the source of record sets; everything customers hold in scope (balances, positions, collateral, recurring charges, as the source of record names them) settled or migrated through the operations back office; care kept through the notice period; dependencies removed. E11 chases settlement daily; a missed settlement date opens P-16 for that scope.
4. E12 completes the closure record from the back office's, identity and engine read-backs, keeping read-only access until it is done.
5. For an entry or a team, A12 then releases the remaining access and allocations and records the receipts. Records are kept, and the team is parked, never deleted (blueprint 5.10).

### P-16 · Incidents and emergency halts
1. **Detection.** Live faults come to E11 continuously, from the platform's and the engineering AIOC's alerts on the line, from E10 and from E4; A4's daily read opens an incident only for a silent degradation no one has flagged.
2. **Severity.** E11 sets it (S1: money or access at risk, or an emergency in an asset or market the entry depends on; S2: a feature broken; S3: a degraded experience; examples are data), opens the record and, for S1 or S2, tells A5 the same minute so every dependent team opens its own. A1 tells the human hub of an S1 the same hour.
3. **Containment** within the response time: E11 invokes the pause or suspension rule the Owner signed, and tells A8 and the operating case the same minute. A suspension of an asset, quotation or market is the approval mechanism's suspension matter, taken at once under that rule and ratified by the matrix's deadline (R7). P-06 is used only later, if an economic value changes.
4. **Customers.** E10 sends the S1 update with the containment, inside the response time; S2 and S3 updates the same day; always from templates A10 has passed, never held by limits. A press statement, if one is needed, is the marketing division's, from E9's facts.
5. **Recovery and resumption.** E12 verifies the recovery evidence and the resumption reference independently. Resumption is decided by the Owner, or for a suspension by the R7 decider, within the severity's response time from verified evidence, with R1's stand-ins for the Owner and the matrix's for R7; it is executed by the operating role that holds the switch, never by whoever suspended. E11 tells A5 the same minute, so dependent teams verify and resume their own scopes. An asset or market that will not return goes into an expedited P-15; E2 opens the follow-up change through P-03; A4 reports it in the weekly review.

## 7. The paths with the other parts

### 7.1 The governing seat and the human hub

- **The line of report** is §3.1: the CPO reports to the human hub; A1 carries it both ways.
- **What stays with the governing seat,** unless it hands it on: a departure from the source of record (R5); the versions §4.5 keeps with it (R6). Its words change the source of record only openly, through the source's own amendment path.
- **What goes to the human hub for its record, the same day:** each seat opened or closed (R4), each position made human or turned back (§4.6), and each delegation a human seat signs, so that who stands behind each seat, and with what share, is always on its register.

### 7.2 The engineering AIOC: the product line

**The rule.** Product Core reaches the engineering AIOC only on the product line between the two parts, with complete product packages (Protocol §18, item 10). Product Core owns the what and the why; the engineering AIOC owns the how and the when.

| Step | Product Core | The engineering AIOC |
|---|---|---|
| Intake standard | writes every package to the engineering AIOC's intake standard: the Product Brief with outcomes, the working-backwards brief, the envelope, the non-functional declarations, Feature Packages with Given/When/Then criteria, the requirements register, the change path (blueprint 5.4, Annex A) | sets the standard |
| Build status | asks it on the line, against pinned code heads; never reads the code host | answers; the answer never blocks the writing |
| Package stores | where an organisation keeps its packages in stores of their own, they are package stores, not code repositories: A8 moves the product side of each package's trail (proposed, product-approved, merged) | moves the engineering side (received, ready or back, planned, building, on demo, done) |
| Sending | after the freeze, the Owner's decision and R6 (P-03): the frozen, content-addressed package (blueprint Annex A.1) | receipt, then verdict: ready, or back with numbered points |
| During the build | every question answered within the organisation's deadline, from the package, decided by the Owner or its COS; at most two exchanges, then the question goes to its source as a decision | plans and builds to its own dates; reports them on the line |
| Demo | E12 runs the first look and acceptance criterion by criterion; the Owner accepts (R1); feedback is a defect against a named criterion, a suggestion, or a change (a new version) | asks for acceptance when on demo |
| Production | launch (R3) waits for the engineering AIOC's own production conditions, such as reconciled tests, an independent review of a money path, proved recovery and its signatures; Product Core never stands in for them | releases |
| Signals | receives production signals and support clusters | sends them on the line |

- Product Core never edits what the engineering AIOC wrote, never speaks to a builder, and never moves the engineering side of the trail; the engineering AIOC never moves the product side.
- **Every tool Product Core needs built** (its Panel aside, which `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` builds) is asked of the engineering AIOC on the line.

### 7.3 The engine division

**Its place.** The engine division (49GAION Engine Room; in an `[ORG]AION`, the part the organisation names) is the one hub for every AI engine and every paid external service. Every unit draws from it through a section of its own, with its own quota. It is demand-led: it supplies a stated need from the best source at the lowest cost that meets it, and never decides the need.

- **Product Core's section.** Every engine and data service of Product Core comes through it: the agents' engines, the reviewer engines, the teams' listening and analytics services, and market data for E5 and A6.
- **One link.** A12 is Product Core's link with the engine division (P-12): it states each need by model kind, level, specification, service and sensitivity class; the engine division allocates, runs and meters. Where an agent needs training on Product Core's own records, A12 states it, and the engine division trains and keeps the model.
- **Spend.** Metered, not capped: reaching the organisation's review mark brings a review to the CPO, never a stop.
- **Never:** a team's own API key, a direct purchase of an engine or a data service, or a secret in any file.
- **Independence needs families.** The section keeps at least three engine families available, so that every checker can run on a family other than the one it checks (§5.6).

### 7.4 The marketing division

**The split.** Product marketing lives in each product team. The organisation's and the companies' marketing stays the marketing division's. Its own mandate governs its side; this section is Product Core's side, sent to it through A1 for its acknowledgement.

## 10. Why this shape

- **A complete team per product,** so that a product never waits on a shared marketing, content, education or support queue, and the team owns its outcome end to end. Product organisations built as one business per product move faster than functional ones, where each product queues for shared functions.
- **One Studio,** because one brand and one design system must hold across every product, and a reviewer that fixes its own findings is no reviewer.
- **A strong CPO Office,** because cross-product effects, economics, claims, packages and agent quality need eyes that no team has: each of A4 to A11 watches or checks what the teams make, and none makes what it checks.
- **Discovery inside the CPO Office,** so that a raw idea is screened, shaped and founded before any team is opened, and a duplicate never becomes a second team.
- **AI in every product,** with its own lead, so that each product keeps adding agents and AI features, and the team improves its own agents.
- **One engine division as the only source,** so that spend is metered once, keys are held once, and engines are bought at the organisation's scale.
- **Delegation, stand-ins and making positions human,** so that no human becomes the queue, nothing waits without a clock, and people can be added anywhere without redesign.
- **Reviewed.** The design was reviewed and scored in five rounds on four engine families; the last round scored it 8, 8, 8.5 and 9 of 10, and its findings are in this text.
- **Not taken, and why:** folding innovation and AI into the Product Manager (each product keeps its own AI lead); merging the Growth Marketer with the Community and Rewards Lead (community and incentives are a craft of their own); sending screen fixes from the Studio back to the teams (the Studio fixes what it finds); one COS for both Studio seats (a COS beside every human seat).
