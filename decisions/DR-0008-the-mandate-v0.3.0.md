## Decision

1. **The mandate takes `v0.3.0`, proposed,** over `v0.2.0` (`decisions/DR-0003`). It governs where it differs from the blueprint (`v0.1.3`); blueprint `v0.2.0` carries it (`MANDATE.md` §11, §12).
2. **§4.3 is rewritten: the Chief of Staff is its seat's own.** A seat's Chief of Staff is the seat's own AI and, to the rest of the part and the organisation, the same party. Every act of the seat, the human acts R1 to R8 included, is its Chief of Staff's to take on the seat's behalf (the one exception: the grant or widening of its own authority is the seat's own hand, which it prepares and records; a standing rule of the seat's unit it may sign on the seat's authority like any act); its one limit is the seat's authority for the act, given **once** on the act itself ("may I do this on your behalf?") or **standing** by a delegation the seat signs (R8), listed, narrowed and withdrawn by the seat alone. The Chief of Staff takes every act as its own first, acts where it holds the authority, and otherwise asks in one line with its recommendation and moves on; the record names the authority; the seat stays accountable; the seat's channels may be read and answered in its name on its authority; a law's or the source of record's requirement of the natural person's own act (which no yes substitutes for), the two-person controls, "a COS never grants or widens its own authority", "a COS acts only for its seat" and the independent checks of §5.6 stay. `v0.2.0`'s delegation becomes the standing authority; its limit "a COS never decides on work it drafted or checked itself" is withdrawn: "nobody checks their own work" is a rule of the checks, not a limit on the Chief of Staff.
3. **§4.2 reads with it:** its lead-in, the R4, R6 and R8 rows and its closing line (a seat's COS acting on its authority is the seat's own hand, never its stand-in; the CPO's stand-in is the human hub); §4.5's lead-in and its third row read the same way.
4. **§4.4 takes the listed chain of stand-ins** (once the list is spent, the act stays pending with the accountable seat and its next date), **the same-holder rule** (a stand-in held by the same human as the lapsed seat is skipped, as the organisation's register of positions says it, received as a yes or a no that names no person) and the rule that **a Chief of Staff acting for its seat where that seat is a stand-in is the seat**, never a stand-in of its own seat. The R6 versions the governing seat keeps have no stand-in.
5. **§4.1 adds the names of a position:** a **short name**, by which the organisation's interfaces introduce it, and a **term**, the initials of the short name's role words with its codes, number and scope kept, by which its Chief of Staff names it wherever it speaks or writes; unique across positions; where initials collide, the CPO sets the term as data before the position is created or made human.
6. **§8 adds four data slots:** the term where initials collide; the channels of a seat its Chief of Staff may read and answer; the measure of the share of a seat's acts its Chief of Staff took itself; the setup period of a Chief of Staff (a mother default of one month).
7. **§4.6, §11 and §12 follow;** §12 adds item 5, the panel's ask card, page of standing authorities and term, for the mother blueprint's panel section at its next version.
8. **Nothing else changes.** The blueprint, the cards, TEST-PC and the Library pin do not change with this record; the blueprint's `v0.2.0` and TEST-PC v2 remain open (§12).

## Why

A Chief of Staff that waits to be handed each act is the queue of its own seat. The first instance initially ran on `v0.2.0`'s rule; its governing seat then defined the Chief of Staff as one that must itself seek to do all the work, get its permission and go, and the instance's panel carries that as one ask card. The mother takes it so that every organisation's Product Core starts there.
